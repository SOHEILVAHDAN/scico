02 — The Landscape

Here, autumn is not simply
a season.
It is a memory.

A landscape of fog, trees, silence and changing light. The forest in Keleston does not surround — it holds, it filters, it remembers. Every October the oaks turn amber and the ground becomes soft with leaves.

03 — Keleston Village

Life here has always been
connected to the land.

To animals, milk, handmade objects and the slow transformation of simple materials into something lasting. Keleston, a small settlement in the highlands north of Shiraz, carries a quiet knowledge passed through hands.

Stone walls built without mortar. Wood dried for three winters. Cheese wrapped in muslin and left in cool cellars. Architecture here was never an image — it was a consequence.

Elevation 1820m / Rain 310mm / Population 423
Primary craft — semi-hard sheep cheese, walnut woodwork
04 — The Memory of Cheese

From milk to cheese.
From material to memory.

A tradition shaped by hands, time and patience. Not folklore for display, but a way of measuring time. The curd cuts at dawn. The cloth drains until noon. The stone presses until evening.

"We do not preserve tradition by copying its shape. We preserve it by continuing its patience."

Milk / Rennet / Cloth / Stone / Wood / Time → Memory

06 — The Form

A low horizontal profile.
A white line in amber.

The building is 32 meters long, 7.2 meters deep, 3.4 meters high. A single monolithic volume, lifted 45 centimeters from the ground on hidden black steel. The elevation reads as pure white plaster, no visible joints.

  • Low horizontal profileReads as horizon, not object
  • Minimal interventionTouch ground only at 3 points
  • Elevated volumeLeaves forest floor undisturbed, air flows
  • Framed viewsEach opening dimensions = forest portrait
  • Deep openings90cm reveal creates shadow, depth, shelter
  • Forest relationshipAll living spaces face north-east oak grove
Structure — Steel frame / White mineral render / Triple glazed oak frames — U value 0.9
07 — Arrival

Arrival is not a moment.
It is a gradual transition
from landscape to architecture.

120m gravel walk through oaks. No gate. No fence. Only a soft crunch of leaves, then white wall.

08 — Living Room

The living room is not an enclosed room. It is a place from which the forest is experienced.

Floor-to-ceiling glazing 6.8m wide. Natural walnut floor, Shiraz stone bench, low neutral linen sofa. Warm light 2700K contrasts with cool blue fog outside. No TV. Only fire and trees.

09 — Modern Kitchen — The Ritual

Here, tradition does not return as decoration.
It returns as a way of living.

The kitchen is not rustic. It is precisely modern — stone island 3.4m, natural walnut cabinetry without handles, minimal Miele appliances hidden. Then: open oak shelving with handmade ceramic vessels from Meymand, woven willow baskets for bread, a refined cheese preparation zone with cool marble and brass tools. Large window frames the forest exactly at worktop height.

StoneShiraz cream limestone, honed, 6cm thick — stays cool for cheese
TimberLocal walnut, oiled, no lacquer — ages with hands
ShelvingSolid oak 34mm, open, adjustable brass pins
VesselsHand-thrown ceramic by Zahra Rostami, Keleston atelier
BasketsWillow woven, keeps sourdough, dried herbs
Cheese ZoneDedicated marble niche — press, drain, salt
Light — 5.2m linear skylight above island, north light only
10 — Bedroom

At night, the house becomes a shelter.
In the morning, the forest becomes the first image.

Walls in warm clay plaster, floor in walnut, bed low to ground. A single framed view 2.4 x 1.8m — autumn oaks, no other distraction. Soft textiles from Yazd, undyed wool. Reading light is a single ceramic pendant, 25cm above bedside stone.

Acoustic — 42db wall build-up / Blackout via 90cm deep reveal + oak shutter, no curtain
