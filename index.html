02 — The Landscape

Here, autumn is not simply
a season.
It is a memory.

Fog, oak, silence and changing light. Every October the ground becomes soft, air carries smoke from walnut wood. The forest does not surround the house — it holds it.

03 — Keleston Village

Life has always been connected to the land.

Animals, milk, handmade objects and slow transformation of simple materials into something lasting. Keleston — 1820m elevation, 423 inhabitants — built without architects, yet with precise knowledge.

Stone walls without mortar. Wood dried 3 winters. Cheese wrapped in muslin, pressed under limestone. Architecture was never an image — it was a consequence of ritual.

Primary craft — semi-hard sheep cheese, walnut woodwork / Rain 310mm / Oak forest 73% cover
04 — Memory of Cheese

From milk to cheese.
From material to memory.

Shaped by hands, time and patience. Curd cut at dawn, cloth drains until noon, stone presses until evening. Tradition not as folklore, but as measure of time.

"We do not preserve tradition by copying its shape. We preserve it by continuing its patience."

Milk / Rennet / Cloth / Stone / Wood / Time → Memory

The house does not compete.
It becomes a quiet line.

Six moves — Subtraction, not addition. A frame through which forest, light and ritual are experienced.

06 — The Form

A low horizontal profile.
A white line in amber.

32m long, lifted on hidden black steel. Pure white mineral render, no visible joints, triple-glazed oak frames. Reads as horizon, not object.

07 — Arrival

Arrival is not a moment.
It is a gradual transition
from landscape to architecture.

120m gravel walk through oaks. No gate. No fence. Only crunch of leaves, then white wall.

08 — Living Room

The living room is not an enclosed room. It is a place from which the forest is experienced.

6.8m floor-to-ceiling glazing. Walnut floor, Shiraz stone bench, low linen sofa. 2700K warm light vs blue fog outside. No TV. Only fire and trees.

09 — Modern Kitchen / Heritage Reinterpreted

Tradition does not return as decoration.
It returns as a way of living.

Sophisticated minimal — NOT rustic. 3.4m Shiraz limestone island, walnut cabinetry without handles, hidden appliances. Open oak shelving 34mm with Meymand handmade ceramics, willow baskets for bread, dedicated cheese zone with cool marble + brass tools. Window at worktop height frames forest precisely.

Stone IslandShiraz cream, honed, 60mm — stays cool for cheese
TimberLocal walnut, oiled, ages with hands
ShelvingSolid oak, brass pins, open display
CeramicHand-thrown by Zahra Rostami, Keleston
BasketsWillow woven — sourdough, dried herbs
Cheese NicheMarble recess — press, drain, salt
10 — Bedroom

At night, shelter.

Clay plaster walls, walnut floor, low bed. Single framed view 2.4x1.8m — only oaks. Yazd undyed wool textiles. One ceramic pendant 25cm above stone bedside.
